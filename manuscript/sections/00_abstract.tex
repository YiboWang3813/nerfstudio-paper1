\begin{abstract}
Reconstructing three-dimensional (3D) volumes from routine two-dimensional (2D)
B-mode ultrasound images is pivotal for volumetric visualization and
quantitative assessment in medical practice.
Within this setting, sensorless methods are promising owing to independence from
external tracking hardware, alleviating deployment complexity and
sterility-related constraints.
Despite recent progress, existing approaches remain limited by per-frame pose
inference and Gaussian pipelines whose image synthesis and adaptation mechanisms
are poorly matched to ultrasound formation physics.
To overcome these bottlenecks, we propose \methodname{}, a Gaussian framework
integrating ultrasound-specific geometry modeling with a physics-aware,
ultrasound-tailored pipeline for fast, high-fidelity sensorless reconstruction.
Specifically, we introduce an Acquisition-constrained Frame Geometry Model
(AFGM), which replaces frame-wise 6-DoF motion estimation with canonical
geometries regularized by acquisition priors for volumetric recovery.
Then, to synthesize physically plausible B-mode images, we develop a
Physics-aware Local-Global Renderer (PLGR), which models finite-slice local echo
formation and path-dependent global transmission in a unified Gaussian-to-image
process.
Furthermore, to adapt Gaussian population management to ultrasound, we design a
Contribution-guided Gaussian Adaptation Strategy (CGAS), which reformulates
densification and pruning according to renderer-derived importance.
Experiments across four ultrasound datasets demonstrate superior performance
over state-of-the-art methods, achieving up to 34.11~dB PSNR and an L1 error of
0.011 on real-world data, with real-time synthesis at 285.10~FPS.
These results establish \methodname{} as a reliable, efficient, and robust
Gaussian solution for sensorless 3D ultrasound reconstruction, with potential
to advance anatomical interpretation and image-guided assessment.
\end{abstract}
