\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

In this work, we present \methodname{}, a Gaussian framework for fast and
high-fidelity sensorless 3D ultrasound reconstruction from 2D B-mode
sweeps.
Its three designs, AFGM, PLGR, and CGAS, address acquisition
geometry, ultrasound-specific rendering, and Gaussian population adaptation,
forming a unified reconstruction pipeline from spatial organization to image
formation and representation control.
AFGM exploits acquisition regularity to replace independent frame-wise
6-DoF motion estimation with a canonical geometry, providing a
spatial basis for volumetric recovery. PLGR couples finite-slice local echo
formation with path-dependent global transmission in a unified
Gaussian-to-image process, while CGAS aligns densification and pruning with
renderer-derived contributions for ultrasound-aware population control.
Extensive experiments across synthetic and real-world datasets demonstrate
superior reconstruction fidelity, robust generalization across
acquisition settings, and real-time rendering efficiency over state-of-the-art
approaches. Still, the geometry model assumes regular sweep progression,
and extending it to more irregular probe trajectories remains future work.
Overall, \methodname{} provides a reliable and efficient Gaussian solution for
sensorless 3D ultrasound reconstruction, supporting volumetric imaging and
quantitative analysis in clinical workflows.
